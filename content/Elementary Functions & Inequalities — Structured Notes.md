## 1. Functions

> [!Note] Function, Domain, Codomain, Range  
> Let $A, B$ be sets.  
> A **function** is a mapping  
> $$
> f: A \to B
> $$
> such that for every $x \in A$, there exists **exactly one** element $f(x) \in B$.
> 
> - $A$: **domain** (set of inputs)  
> - $B$: **codomain** (set of possible outputs)  
> - $\mathrm{Range}(f)=\{f(x)\mid x\in A\}$  
> 
> **Notation:**  
> $$
> f: x \mapsto f(x)
> $$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Functions  
> - Always check domain restrictions (denominators, square roots, logarithms).  
> - Verify domain assumptions before testing properties (odd/even, monotonicity).

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Functions  
> 1. Determine the domain and range of $f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-2}}$.  
> 2. Give an example of a mapping that is not a function.

## 2. Interval Notation

> [!Note] Intervals  
> Let $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ with $a<b$.
> 
> - $(a,b)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid a<x<b\}$  
> - $(a,b]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid a<x\le b\}$  
> - $[a,b)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid a\le x<b\}$  
> - $[a,b]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid a\le x\le b\}$

> [!Note] Properties — Intervals  
> - Parentheses $(\ )$: open endpoint  
> - Brackets $[\ ]$: closed endpoint

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Intervals  
> - Convert interval notation into inequalities before solving.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Intervals  
> 1. Express $[-2,3)$ using inequalities.  
> 2. Solve $|x-1|<2$ and write the solution in interval form.

## 3. Exponential Conventions

> [!Note] Special Powers  
> Let $x\in\mathbb{R}$.
> 
> - $x^0=1 \ (x\ne0)$  
> - $0^x=0 \ (x>0)$  
> - $0^0$ is undefined

## 4. Absolute Value

> [!Note] Absolute Value  
> For $x\in\mathbb{R}$,
> $$
> |x|=
> \begin{cases}
> x, & x\ge0, \\
> -x, & x<0.
> \end{cases}
> $$

> [!Note] Properties — Absolute Value  
> - $|x|\ge0$  
> - $|x|=|-x|$  
> - $|x|^2=x^2$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Absolute Value  
> - Split into cases based on the sign inside $|\cdot|$.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Absolute Value  
> 1. Solve $|2x-1|=3$.  
> 2. Solve $|x-2|+|x+1|=5$.

## 6. Inequalities

> [!Note] Quadratic Inequality  
> Let
> $$
> f(x)=x^2+2x+4=(x+1)^2+3.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> f(x)\ge3 \quad \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.
> $$

> [!Note] Fundamental Property  
> - $x^2\ge0$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Inequalities  
> - Completing the square reveals extrema.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Inequalities  
> 1. Find the minimum of $x^2-4x+7$.  
> 2. Solve $x^2+2x+4<5$.

## 7. Odd and Even Functions

> [!Note] Even and Odd Functions  
> Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$.
> 
> - Even: $f(-x)=f(x)$  
> - Odd: $f(-x)=-f(x)$

> [!Note] Geometric Interpretation  
> - Even: symmetry about the $Oy$ axis  
> - Odd: symmetry about the origin

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Parity  
> - Compute $f(x)$ and $f(-x)$ and compare.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Parity  
> 1. Determine the parity of $f(x)=x^4-x^2$.  
> 2. Prove that the sum of two odd functions is odd.

## 8. Monotonicity

> [!Note] Increasing and Decreasing Functions  
> Let $f$ be defined on an interval $I$.
> 
> - Increasing: $x>y \Rightarrow f(x)>f(y)$  
> - Decreasing: $x>y \Rightarrow f(x)<f(y)$

> [!Note] Calculus Criterion  
> - $f'(x)>0 \Rightarrow f$ increasing  
> - $f'(x)<0 \Rightarrow f$ decreasing

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Monotonicity  
> - The sign of $f'(x)$ controls monotonicity.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Monotonicity  
> 1. Study monotonicity of $f(x)=x^3-3x$.  
> 2. Find intervals of increase/decrease of $f(x)=\ln x$.

## 9. Power Functions

> [!Note] Power Function  
> A power function has the form
> $$
> f(x)=k x^a,
> $$
> where $k,a\in\mathbb{R}$.

> [!Note] Properties — Power Functions  
> - Domain depends on $a$  
> - Symmetry depends on parity (tính chẵn, lẻ) of $a$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Power Functions  
> - Fractional and negative exponents restrict the domain.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Power Functions  
> 1. Sketch $y=x^{1/3}$.  
> 2. Determine the domain of $y=x^{-2}$.

## 10. Logarithms

> [!Note] Logarithm  
> Let $a>0$, $a\ne1$, and $b>0$.
> $$
> \log_a b=c \iff a^c=b.
> $$

> [!Note] Properties — Logarithms  
> - $\log(ab)=\log a+\log b$  
> - $\log(a/b)=\log a-\log b$  
> - $\log a^k=k\log a$  
> - Change of base:
> $$
> \log_a b=\log_a c\cdot\log_c b
> $$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Logarithms  
> - Rewrite sums as products.  
> - Insert intermediate bases when helpful.

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Logarithms  
> 1. Prove $\log_2 5\cdot\log_5 2=1$.  
> 2. Solve $\log_2(x-1)=3$.
