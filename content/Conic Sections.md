## Conic Sections (Basic Curves)

> [!Note] Overview — Conic Sections  
> **Conic sections** are curves obtained by intersecting a plane with a cone.
> 
> The four basic types studied in high school are:
> - **Line**
> - **Circle**
> - **Parabola**
> - **Ellipse**
> - **Hyperbola**

They appear frequently in **algebra, geometry, physics**, and **analytic geometry**.

---

> [!Note] Line  
> A **line** is the simplest curve, representing a constant rate of change.
> 
> Common forms:
> - **Slope–intercept form:**
>   $$
>   y=mx+b
>   $$
>   where $m$ is the slope, $b$ is the $y$-intercept.
> 
> - **General form:**
>   $$
>   ax+by+c=0
>   $$

> [!Note] Properties — Line  
> - Straight, infinite in both directions  
> - Constant slope  
> - Degree 1 equation

---

> [!Note] Circle  
> A **circle** is the set of all points in a plane at a fixed distance from a fixed point.
> 
> - Center: $(h,k)$  
> - Radius: $r$
> 
> **Standard equation:**
> $$
> (x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2
> $$

> [!Note] Properties — Circle  
> - Perfect symmetry  
> - All radii are equal  
> - Degree 2 equation  
> - Special case of an ellipse

---

> [!Note] Parabola  
> A **parabola** is the set of points equidistant from:
> - a fixed point (**focus**)  
> - a fixed line (**directrix**)
> 
> **Standard forms:**
> - Vertical axis:
>   $$
>   (x-h)^2=4p(y-k)
>   $$
> - Horizontal axis:
>   $$
>   (y-k)^2=4p(x-h)
>   $$

> [!Note] Properties — Parabola  
> - Opens upward, downward, left, or right  
> - Has **one focus**  
> - Symmetric about its axis  
> - Degree 2 equation

---

> [!Note] Ellipse  
> An **ellipse** is the set of points such that the **sum of distances** to two fixed points (foci) is constant.
> 
> **Standard forms:**
> - Major axis horizontal:
>   $$
>   \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
>   $$
> - Major axis vertical:
>   $$
>   \frac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{a^2}=1
>   $$

> [!Note] Properties — Ellipse  
> - Two foci  
> - Closed curve  
> - Circle is a special ellipse with $a=b$  
> - Degree 2 equation

---

> [!Note] Hyperbola  
> A **hyperbola** is the set of points such that the **absolute difference of distances** to two fixed points (foci) is constant.
> 
> **Standard forms:**
> - Horizontal transverse axis:
>   $$
>   \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
>   $$
> - Vertical transverse axis:
>   $$
>   \frac{(y-k)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1
>   $$

> [!Note] Properties — Hyperbola  
> - Two separate branches  
> - Two foci  
> - Has asymptotes  
> - Degree 2 equation

---

> [!Note] Quick Comparison — Conic Sections  

| Curve      | Distance Property | Shape        | Closed? |
|------------|------------------|--------------|---------|
| Line       | Linear relation  | Straight     | No      |
| Circle     | Fixed distance   | Round        | Yes     |
| Parabola   | Equal distance   | U-shaped     | No      |
| Ellipse    | Sum of distances | Oval         | Yes     |
| Hyperbola  | Difference       | Two branches | No      |

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Conics  
> - Complete the square to identify the curve.  
> - Degree 2 equations represent **conic sections**.  
> - Look at the **signs** ($+$ or $-$) to distinguish ellipse vs hyperbola.  
> - Check symmetry to determine orientation.
