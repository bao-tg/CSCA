## Basic Analytical Geometry

> [!Note] Point  
> A **point** in the Cartesian plane is represented by an ordered pair
> $$
> P(x,y),
> $$
> where:
> - $x$ is the **abscissa** (horizontal coordinate),
> - $y$ is the **ordinate** (vertical coordinate).
> 
> The set of all points $(x,y)$ forms the **Cartesian plane** $\mathbb{R}^2$.

---

> [!Note] Line  
> A **line** in the Cartesian plane is the set of points $(x,y)$ satisfying a linear equation
> $$
> ax+by+c=0,
> $$
> where $a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}$ and $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$.
> 
> Common forms:
> - **Slope–intercept form:**  
>   $$
>   y=mx+b
>   $$
>   where $m$ is the slope.
> - **Point–slope form:**  
>   $$
>   y-y_0=m(x-x_0).
>   $$

> [!Note] Properties — Line  
> - Two distinct points determine a unique line.  
> - Parallel lines have equal slopes.  
> - Perpendicular lines satisfy $m_1m_2=-1$ (when slopes exist).

---

> [!Note] Graph of a Function  
> Let $f:A\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$.  
> The **graph** of $f$ is the set
> $$
> \Gamma_f=\{(x,f(x))\mid x\in A\}\subset\mathbb{R}^2.
> $$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Graphs  
> - Each vertical line intersects the graph of a function at **most one point**.  
> - Domain restrictions appear as gaps or boundaries on the graph.

---

> [!Note] Point on a Graph  
> A point $P(x_0,y_0)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$ **if and only if**
> $$
> y_0=f(x_0).
> $$

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Point on Graph  
> - Substitute $x_0$ into $f(x)$ and compare with $y_0$.  
> - This criterion is often used to determine unknown parameters.

---

> [!Note] Symmetry in the Cartesian Plane  
> Let $P(x,y)$ be a point in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
> 
> - Symmetry about the $Ox$ axis: $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$  
> - Symmetry about the $Oy$ axis: $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$  
> - Symmetry about the origin: $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$

> [!Note] Symmetry of Graphs  
> Let $y=f(x)$.
> 
> - Symmetry about $Oy$  $\iff f(-x)=f(x)$ (even function)  
> - Symmetry about the origin $\iff f(-x)=-f(x)$ (odd function)

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Symmetry  
> - Check symmetry algebraically using $f(-x)$.  
> - Symmetry reduces computation and simplifies graph sketching.

---

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Analytical Geometry  
> 1. Find the equation of the line passing through $(1,2)$ and $(3,-1)$.  
> 2. Determine whether the point $(2,5)$ lies on the graph of $y=2x+1$.  
> 3. Show that the graph of $y=x^2$ is symmetric about the $Oy$ axis.  
> 4. Find the image of the point $(3,-4)$ under symmetry about the origin.
