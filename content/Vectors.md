## Vectors

> [!Note] Vector  
> A **vector** is a quantity that has both **magnitude** (length) and **direction**.
> 
> Examples:
> - Displacement  
> - Velocity  
> - Force
> 
> Vectors are usually written as:
> - $\vec{a}$ (arrow notation)  
> - $\mathbf{a}$ (bold notation)

---

> [!Note] Representation of Vectors  
> In the plane, a vector can be represented by its **components**:
> $$
> \vec{a}=(a_x,a_y)
> $$
> 
> In space:
> $$
> \vec{a}=(a_x,a_y,a_z)
> $$

> [!Note] Position Vector  
> The **position vector** of a point $A(x,y)$ is
> $$
> \vec{OA}=(x,y)
> $$

---

> [!Note] Magnitude of a Vector  
> The **magnitude** (length) of a vector $\vec{a}=(a_x,a_y)$ is
> $$
> |\vec{a}|=\sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Equality of Vectors  
> Two vectors are **equal** if:
> - They have the same magnitude  
> - They have the same direction
> 
> Their initial points do not matter.

---

> [!Note] Vector Addition  
> For $\vec{a}=(a_x,a_y)$ and $\vec{b}=(b_x,b_y)$:
> $$
> \vec{a}+\vec{b}=(a_x+b_x,\ a_y+b_y)
> $$

> [!Note] Geometric Interpretation  
> - **Triangle rule**  
> - **Parallelogram rule**

---

> [!Note] Scalar Multiplication  
> For a real number $k$:
> $$
> k\vec{a}=(ka_x,ka_y)
> $$
> 
> - If $k>0$: same direction  
> - If $k<0$: opposite direction  
> - If $|k|>1$: longer vector

---

> [!Note] Unit Vector  
> A **unit vector** has magnitude $1$.
> 
> The unit vector in the direction of $\vec{a}$ is
> $$
> \hat{a}=\frac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|}, \quad \vec{a}\ne\vec{0}
> $$

> [!Note] Standard Unit Vectors  
> - $\vec{i}=(1,0)$  
> - $\vec{j}=(0,1)$  
> - $\vec{k}=(0,0,1)$

---

> [!Note] Dot Product (Scalar Product)  
> The **dot product** of $\vec{a}=(a_x,a_y)$ and $\vec{b}=(b_x,b_y)$ is
> $$
> \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=a_xb_x+a_yb_y
> $$

> [!Note] Geometric Meaning — Dot Product  
> $$
> \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta
> $$
> where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors.

> [!Note] Applications  
> - Find angles between vectors  
> - Check orthogonality: $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=0$

---

> [!Note] Projection of a Vector  
> The projection of $\vec{a}$ onto $\vec{b}$ is
> $$
> \text{proj}_{\vec{b}}\vec{a}
> =\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|^2}\vec{b}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Collinearity and Orthogonality  
> - Vectors $\vec{a},\vec{b}$ are **collinear** if $\vec{a}=k\vec{b}$  
> - Vectors $\vec{a},\vec{b}$ are **perpendicular** if $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=0$

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Vectors  
> - Always start by writing vectors in **component form**.  
> - Use the dot product to handle angles and perpendicularity.  
> - Normalize vectors when direction is required.  
> - Draw a diagram to understand direction and geometry.
