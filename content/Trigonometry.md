## Trigonometry in the Cartesian Plane

> [!Note] Angle in Standard Position  
> An angle $\theta$ is said to be in **standard position** if:
> - Its vertex is at the origin $O(0,0)$,
> - Its initial side lies along the positive $x$-axis,
> - Its terminal side is obtained by rotating the initial side counterclockwise.

---

> [!Note] Point on the Terminal Side  
> If the terminal side of an angle $\theta$ passes through a point
> $$
> P(x,y)\neq(0,0),
> $$
> then the coordinates $(x,y)$ determine the trigonometric ratios of $\theta$.

---

> [!Note] Definition of Tangent  
> For an angle $\theta$ in standard position whose terminal side passes through $P(x,y)$ with $x\ne0$,
> $$
> \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}.
> $$

> [!Note] Consequence  
> Knowing $\tan\theta$ and one coordinate of a point on the terminal side allows us to determine the other coordinate up to sign constraints.

---

> [!Note] Quadrants and Signs  
> The sign of trigonometric functions depends on the **quadrant** in which the terminal side lies:
> 
> - Quadrant I $(x>0,y>0)$: $\sin\theta>0,\ \cos\theta>0,\ \tan\theta>0$  
> - Quadrant II $(x<0,y>0)$: $\sin\theta>0,\ \cos\theta<0,\ \tan\theta<0$  
> - Quadrant III $(x<0,y<0)$: $\sin\theta<0,\ \cos\theta<0,\ \tan\theta>0$  
> - Quadrant IV $(x>0,y<0)$: $\sin\theta<0,\ \cos\theta>0,\ \tan\theta<0$

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Strategy — Coordinate Trigonometry  
> 1. Use $\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}$ to relate coordinates.  
> 2. Use the sign of $x$ and the given value of $\tan\theta$ to determine the sign of $y$.  
> 3. Check consistency with the quadrant implied by the point.

---

> [!Note] Typical Application  
> If the terminal side of $\theta$ passes through $P(x,y)$ and $\tan\theta$ is known:
> $$
> y = x\tan\theta.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Additional Practice Problems  
> 1. The terminal side of $\theta$ passes through $(4,y)$ and $\tan\theta=-\frac{3}{2}$. Find $y$.  
> 2. A point $P(x,-6)$ lies on the terminal side of $\theta$ with $\tan\theta=2$. Determine $x$.  
> 3. Determine the quadrant of $\theta$ if $\tan\theta>0$ and $x<0$.  
> 4. Find $\tan\theta$ if the terminal side passes through $(-5,2)$.


## Basic trigonometric identities


> [!Note] Right Triangle Definitions  
> Let $\theta$ be an acute angle in a right triangle.  
> Denote:
> - Opposite side: $O$
> - Adjacent side: $A$
> - Hypotenuse: $H$
> 
> The basic trigonometric ratios are defined as:
> $$
> \sin\theta=\frac{O}{H},\quad
> \cos\theta=\frac{A}{H},\quad
> \tan\theta=\frac{O}{A}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions  
> The reciprocal identities are:
> $$
> \csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta},\quad
> \sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta},\quad
> \cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Pythagorean Identities  
> The fundamental Pythagorean identities are:
> $$
> \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1,
> $$
> $$
> 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta,
> $$
> $$
> 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta.
> $$

> [!Note] Origin  
> These identities follow from the Pythagorean theorem
> $$
> O^2+A^2=H^2.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Quotient Identities  
> The quotient identities relate tangent and cotangent to sine and cosine:
> $$
> \tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\quad
> \cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Even–Odd Properties  
> For all $\theta$ where defined:
> $$
> \sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta,\quad
> \cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta,\quad
> \tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Cofunction Identities  
> For complementary angles $\theta$ and $\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta$:
> $$
> \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\cos\theta,
> $$
> $$
> \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\sin\theta,
> $$
> $$
> \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\cot\theta.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Trigonometric Identities  
> - Express all trigonometric functions in terms of $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ when simplifying.  
> - Use Pythagorean identities to eliminate squared terms.  
> - Verify identities by simplifying **one side only**, never both.

## Trigonometry on the Unit Circle

> [!Note] Unit Circle  
> The **unit circle** is the circle in the Cartesian plane defined by
> $$
> x^2+y^2=1,
> $$
> centered at the origin $O(0,0)$.
> 
> Each real number $\theta$ corresponds to a point
> $$
> P(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
> $$
> on the unit circle.

---

> [!Note] Trigonometric Functions via the Unit Circle  
> Let $\theta$ be an angle in standard position and let
> $$
> P(x,y)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
> $$
> be the point where the terminal side of $\theta$ intersects the unit circle.
> 
> Then:
> $$
> \sin\theta=y,\quad
> \cos\theta=x,\quad
> \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}\ (x\ne0).
> $$

---

> [!Note] Pythagorean Identity from the Unit Circle  
> Since every point on the unit circle satisfies $x^2+y^2=1$, it follows that
> $$
> \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Remaining Trigonometric Functions  
> For $P(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$:
> $$
> \sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\ (\cos\theta\ne0),
> $$
> $$
> \csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\ (\sin\theta\ne0),
> $$
> $$
> \cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\ (\sin\theta\ne0).
> $$

---

> [!Note] Quadrants and Signs on the Unit Circle  
> The sign of trigonometric functions depends on the quadrant of $P(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$:
> 
> - Quadrant I: $\sin\theta>0,\ \cos\theta>0,\ \tan\theta>0$  
> - Quadrant II: $\sin\theta>0,\ \cos\theta<0,\ \tan\theta<0$  
> - Quadrant III: $\sin\theta<0,\ \cos\theta<0,\ \tan\theta>0$  
> - Quadrant IV: $\sin\theta<0,\ \cos\theta>0,\ \tan\theta<0$

---

> [!Note] Symmetry on the Unit Circle  
> Let $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$.
> 
> - $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$  
> - $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$  
> - $\tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta$
> 
> These follow from symmetry of the unit circle about the $x$-axis and $y$-axis.

---

> [!Note] Reference Angles  
> For any angle $\theta$, the **reference angle** $\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]$ is the acute angle between the terminal side of $\theta$ and the $x$-axis.
> 
> The values of $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ have the same magnitude as those of $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$, with signs determined by the quadrant.

---

> [!Note] Special Angles on the Unit Circle  
> The exact trigonometric values for common angles are:
> 
> - $\theta=0$: $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(1,0)$  
> - $\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}$: $(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{1}{2})$  
> - $\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}$: $(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})$  
> - $\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}$: $(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$  
> - $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$: $(0,1)$

---

> [!Note] Periodicity  
> Trigonometric functions are periodic:
> $$
> \sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta,\quad
> \cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta,\quad
> \tan(\theta+\pi)=\tan\theta.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Unit Circle  
> - Reduce angles modulo $2\pi$ before evaluation.  
> - Determine the quadrant first to fix signs.  
> - Use symmetry to compute values quickly.

---

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Unit Circle

## Multiplication Formulas in Trigonometry

> [!Note] Angle Addition and Subtraction  
> For all angles $\alpha,\beta$,
> $$
> \sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\pm\cos\alpha\sin\beta,
> $$
> $$
> \cos(\alpha\pm\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta\mp\sin\alpha\sin\beta,
> $$
> $$
> \tan(\alpha\pm\beta)=\frac{\tan\alpha\pm\tan\beta}{1\mp\tan\alpha\tan\beta}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Double-Angle Formulas  
> For any angle $\alpha$,
> $$
> \sin 2\alpha=2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha,
> $$
> $$
> \cos 2\alpha=\cos^2\alpha-\sin^2\alpha
> =2\cos^2\alpha-1
> =1-2\sin^2\alpha,
> $$
> $$
> \tan 2\alpha=\frac{2\tan\alpha}{1-\tan^2\alpha}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Triple-Angle Formulas  
> For any angle $\alpha$,
> $$
> \sin 3\alpha=3\sin\alpha-4\sin^3\alpha,
> $$
> $$
> \cos 3\alpha=4\cos^3\alpha-3\cos\alpha.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Product-to-Sum Formulas  
> For all angles $\alpha,\beta$,
> $$
> \sin\alpha\sin\beta=\frac{1}{2}\big[\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos(\alpha+\beta)\big],
> $$
> $$
> \cos\alpha\cos\beta=\frac{1}{2}\big[\cos(\alpha-\beta)+\cos(\alpha+\beta)\big],
> $$
> $$
> \sin\alpha\cos\beta=\frac{1}{2}\big[\sin(\alpha+\beta)+\sin(\alpha-\beta)\big].
> $$

---

> [!Note] Sum-to-Product Formulas  
> For all angles $\alpha,\beta$,
> $$
> \sin\alpha+\sin\beta
> =2\sin\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2},
> $$
> $$
> \sin\alpha-\sin\beta
> =2\cos\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2},
> $$
> $$
> \cos\alpha+\cos\beta
> =2\cos\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2},
> $$
> $$
> \cos\alpha-\cos\beta
> =-2\sin\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}.
> $$

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Multiplication Formulas  
> - Use **product-to-sum** to simplify integrals and equations.  
> - Use **sum-to-product** to factor trigonometric expressions.  
> - Rewrite everything in terms of $\sin$ and $\cos$ when proving identities.  
> - Check domain restrictions when using $\tan$ formulas.
