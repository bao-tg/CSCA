## Distance Formulas

> [!Note] Distance Concept  
> In geometry, **distance** measures the shortest length between objects
> (points, lines, planes, circles).
> 
> Most distance formulas are derived from the **Pythagorean theorem**.

---

> [!Note] Distance Between Two Points (Plane)  
> For points $A(x_1,y_1)$ and $B(x_2,y_2)$:
> $$
> AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Distance Between Two Points (Space)  
> For points $A(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ and $B(x_2,y_2,z_2)$:
> $$
> AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Distance from a Point to a Line  
> Distance from point $P(x_0,y_0)$ to line
> $$
> ax+by+c=0
> $$
> is
> $$
> d=\frac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}
> $$

VD: $P(3, 4)$, $3x + 4y + 5 = 0$

$$d = \frac{30}{5}$$

---

> [!Note] Distance from a Point to a Plane  
> Distance from point $P(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ to plane
> $$
> Ax+By+Cz+D=0
> $$
> is
> $$
> d=\frac{|Ax_0+By_0+Cz_0+D|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2}}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Distance Between Two Parallel Lines  
> For parallel lines
> $$
> ax+by+c_1=0,\quad ax+by+c_2=0
> $$
> distance is
> $$
> d=\frac{|c_2-c_1|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Distance Between Two Parallel Planes  
> For parallel planes
> $$
> Ax+By+Cz+D_1=0,\quad Ax+By+Cz+D_2=0
> $$
> distance is
> $$
> d=\frac{|D_2-D_1|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2}}
> $$

---

> [!Note] Distance from a Point to a Circle  
> For circle with center $O(x_0,y_0)$ and radius $r$:
> 
> Distance from point $P(x,y)$ to the **circle** is
> $$
> d=\big|PO-r\big|
> $$

> [!Note] Interpretation  
> - $d=0$: point lies on the circle  
> - $d>0$: shortest gap between point and circle

---

> [!Note] Distance Between Two Circles  
> For two circles with centers $O_1,O_2$ and radii $r_1,r_2$:
> $$
> d=\big|O_1O_2-(r_1+r_2)\big|
> $$

> [!Note] Special Cases  
> - $O_1O_2>r_1+r_2$: circles are separate  
> - $O_1O_2=r_1+r_2$: externally tangent  
> - $|r_1-r_2|<O_1O_2<r_1+r_2$: intersect  
> - $O_1O_2=|r_1-r_2|$: internally tangent  

---

> [!Note] Distance Between a Point and a Segment  
> Distance from point $P$ to segment $AB$:
> - Drop a perpendicular if projection lies on segment  
> - Otherwise, distance is $\min(PA,PB)$

---

> [!Note] Key Insight — Why Absolute Values Appear  
> Distance is always **non-negative**, so absolute values ensure correctness
> regardless of orientation or sign.

---

> [!Note] Problem-Solving Insights — Distance  
> - Always write the equation in **standard form** before applying formulas.  
> - Check whether objects are parallel first.  
> - Use geometry to decide which distance formula applies.  
> - Draw a quick sketch to avoid conceptual mistakes.

---

> [!Note] Additional Problems — Distance Formulas  
> 1. Find the distance between $(1,2)$ and $(4,-2)$.  
> 2. Find the distance from $(3,1)$ to the line $2x-y+5=0$.  
> 3. Find the distance from $(1,2,3)$ to the plane $x-2y+2z-4=0$.  
> 4. Determine the distance between the parallel lines $3x+4y-1=0$ and $3x+4y+7=0$.  
> 5. Find the distance between two circles $x^2+y^2=4$ and $(x-5)^2+y^2=1$.
